CYL Pharmaceutics + Delivery Lab

at Taipei Medical University, School of Pharmacy · 藥劑學與先進藥物遞送系統實驗室

We design nanocarriers and novel formulations to deliver drugs where they are needed. We aim to improve therapeutic efficacy and overcome drug delivery challenges.

Nanoparticles

Nanoparticles are carriers tens to hundreds of nanometres across. A typical polymer particle has a drug-loaded core, a polymer matrix that holds the cargo, and a surface coating of PEG chains and targeting ligands.

Why nanoparticles?

Packaging a drug in a nanoparticle changes how it behaves in the body. Nanosizing and a hydrophilic coating help poorly soluble drugs disperse in water; small size favours uptake across membranes; surface ligands bind receptors on target cells; and the surface charge (zeta potential) keeps particles apart and tunes how they meet cells.

PLGA

As a new laboratory we keep our scope open. The excipient we reach for most often is PLGA, poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid): long chains of lactic and glycolic acid units. It breaks down into natural metabolites and is used in many approved medical products.

Controlled release

Water slowly cleaves the ester bonds, so the particle erodes over days to months and the drug leaves by diffusing out and as the matrix breaks down. The lactic : glycolic ratio and the chain length tune the speed.
